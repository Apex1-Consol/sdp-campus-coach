Accreditation and readiness

What does readiness mean?

Accreditation is not only about having a certificate or submitting an application. A campus must be able to show that it is legally, operationally, financially and practically ready to deliver the specific programme applied for.

1. Institutional compliance

This concerns the organisation itself: legal registration, applicable tax compliance, financial sustainability, governance, policies and the ability to operate lawfully.

2. Programme delivery readiness

This concerns whether the campus can actually deliver the programme: suitable premises, current lease or proof of ownership, appropriate learning resources, equipment, staff capacity, learner support and documented delivery processes.

The manager’s evidence habit

For every programme, keep a readiness file that identifies the requirement, the evidence, the owner, the expiry or review date, and the gap that still needs action.

Common audit risks:
  • Expired leases, certificates or staff records
  • Equipment that does not match the curriculum requirements
  • Policies that exist but are not followed in practice
  • Missing learner, attendance, assessment or moderation evidence
  • Applying for a programme the campus cannot currently deliver

Use the current official requirements for the exact qualification and programme. Start with the QCTO guidance for skills development providers .
